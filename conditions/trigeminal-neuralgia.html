Trigeminal Neuralgia

Trigeminal neuralgia sends sudden, electric-shock pain across one side of the face—often set off by a breeze, a shave, or a smile.

Understanding Trigeminal Neuralgia

Trigeminal neuralgia is a nerve pain disorder involving the trigeminal nerve, which carries sensation from your face to your brain. The pain arrives in sudden, intense bursts—often described as stabbing, burning, or like an electric shock—and everyday movements such as chewing, talking, or washing your face can set it off. Our neurologists diagnose it through a careful history and neurological exam, use advanced imaging to check for a blood vessel or other cause pressing on the nerve, and rule out conditions that can mimic it.

⚠ New facial drooping, weakness, numbness, or slurred speech can signal a stroke—call 911 right away.

Signs it's time to see a neurologist

  • Sudden, shock-like or stabbing pain on one side of the face
  • Attacks triggered by light touch—chewing, talking, shaving, or a cool breeze
  • Brief, repeated bursts of pain that come and go over days or weeks
  • Pain centered around the cheek, jaw, teeth, or eye
  • Over-the-counter pain relievers that no longer bring relief
  • Facial pain that is spreading, constant, or paired with numbness or weakness

Our Approach

How We Treat Trigeminal Neuralgia

01

Pinpointing the true source of the pain

We begin with a detailed history and a focused neurological exam to map exactly where your pain occurs and what provokes it. Advanced imaging helps us see whether a blood vessel or another condition is pressing on the trigeminal nerve, and the nerve and muscle expertise of Reed Stone, MD, helps distinguish trigeminal neuralgia from other causes of facial and nerve pain.

02

A plan tailored to your pain

Many people gain meaningful relief once the misfiring nerve signals behind the attacks are calmed. When facial pain overlaps with migraine or proves stubborn, our headache and neuromodulation specialists—including Arnaldo Da Silva, MD, Co-Director of the Palm Beach Headache Center, and Paul Winner, DO—can weigh further options and adjust your plan as symptoms change.

03

Research-informed care over the long run

Because our physicians stay active in neurology research through the on-site Premiere Research Institute, your care reflects current thinking in the field. We follow you over time—refining your treatment, tracking your response, and coordinating any additional care if the pain persists—so you can get back to eating, talking, and living without bracing for the next jolt.
